# Project: AI Solutions Portfolio Website

## 1. Objective

Build a polished, modern, responsive personal AI portfolio website showcasing four AI solutions I have helped design, configure and implement.

The purpose of the website is to demonstrate my practical experience in:
- Identifying business problems and assessing AI opportunities.
- Evaluating time-consuming processes and potential business impact.
- Designing and building AI solutions.
- Configuring integrations between AI models, business applications, databases and automation platforms.
- Assessing implementation risks and governance requirements.
- Supporting user acceptance testing, end-user adoption and continuous improvement.

The portfolio is intended for recruiters and hiring managers recruiting for AI Implementation, AI Enablement, AI Workflow Automation and AI Transformation roles.

**The central message is that I do more than use AI tools: I identify operational problems, build practical solutions, connect systems and support successful implementation.**

Build the actual website, not just a design concept or static mockup.

## 2. Design direction

Use the attached portfolio screenshot as the primary visual reference.

Recreate its overall design language:
- Clean, professional technology portfolio.
- White or very light grey background.
- Dark navy typography.
- Blue and purple accents, with green accents for business impact.
- Subtle gradients and borders.
- Rounded project cards.
- Generous spacing without excessive whitespace.
- Modern sans-serif typography.
- Simple, consistent icons.
- Clear visual hierarchy.
- Minimal animations and hover effects.

The result should look credible as a professional portfolio for someone working at the intersection of business operations, AI implementation and workflow automation.

Avoid:
- Generic AI imagery and robot illustrations.
- Excessive gradients or glowing effects.
- Large blocks of text.
- Unnecessary animations.
- Overly complex navigation.
- A generic template-like appearance.
- Fabricated customer logos, testimonials or credentials.

Prioritise readability, professional presentation and fast access to the actual solutions.

## 3. Website structure

Build a single-page website with four main areas:

1. Navigation bar.
2. Hero section and implementation approach.
3. Four AI solution cards.
4. Footer with CV and contact links.

All four solutions should appear on the same landing page.

Use a two-column grid on desktop, with two cards per row. On mobile, stack the cards vertically.

The landing page should be concise enough to scan in approximately 60–120 seconds.

## 4. Navigation bar

Create a simple sticky navigation bar containing:

- Initials or a small personal monogram on the left.
- "AI SOLUTIONS PORTFOLIO" branding.
- Solutions.
- Approach.
- About.
- Resume button on the right.

The navigation links should scroll to the relevant sections.

The Resume button should link to a configurable CV file or URL. Do not invent a CV URL.

The About link can point to a short introductory section or footer rather than a separate page.

Keep the navigation compact and unobtrusive.

## 6. Solution cards

Create four detailed but concise cards.

Each card must contain:

1. Solution number and category.
2. Solution title.
3. One-sentence description.
4. Business impact, where figures are available.
5. A horizontal or compact workflow diagram.
6. My role.
7. Risk and governance.
8. Technology tags.
9. A "View full details" interaction.

The cards must communicate my personal contribution, not merely describe what the tools do.

Use clear icons and small labelled workflow nodes connected by arrows. Make the workflow understandable without requiring visitors to read lengthy descriptions.

## 8. Interactive implementation details

The "View full details" link should open a functional modal or expandable panel.

Do not create additional webpages for each solution.

The expanded view should contain:

### Business problem
Explain the original process, pain point and business impact.

### Solution architecture
Display a larger workflow diagram explaining how the systems connect.

### Implementation
Explain what I configured and built, including integration logic, data flow and relevant technical decisions.

### Risk and governance
Explain the risks assessed, the safeguards implemented, the role of human review and any relevant approval requirements.

### Adoption and continuous improvement
Explain testing, user guidance, feedback and subsequent improvements.

### Impact and lessons learned
Present the available results, distinguishing measured outcomes from estimates and potential benefits.

Use only information supported by the supplied portfolio materials. Where further details are missing, create clearly labelled placeholders for me to complete rather than inventing technical information.

The modal must have a close button, work with keyboard navigation and be usable on mobile.

## 11. Technical requirements

Use the existing project structure and framework if one is already present.

If starting from scratch, use a lightweight, maintainable React implementation with TypeScript and a suitable styling approach.

Requirements:
- Responsive desktop, tablet and mobile layouts.
- Reusable solution-card components.
- Reusable workflow-diagram components.
- Reusable technology tags.
- Functional navigation and anchor links.
- Functional detail modals or expandable panels.
- Accessible semantic HTML.
- Keyboard-accessible interactions.
- Appropriate colour contrast.
- Optimised images and icons.
- Minimal dependencies.
- Fast loading.
- SEO-friendly page title and description.
- No unnecessary backend or database.
- No exposed API keys or credentials.

Use an appropriate icon library if one is already installed. Otherwise, choose a lightweight option.

Use the supplied workflow images as the source of truth for the technical architecture. Do not substitute a generic AI workflow where an actual workflow has been provided.

The workflow images may be used as visual previews, but ensure the cards themselves remain readable and responsive.

## 12. Confidentiality and accuracy

Do not display the names, logos or identifying references of the enterprise organisations where the work was performed.

Use the solution categories specified above.

Do not include confidential company data, proprietary code, credentials, personal information or sensitive internal architecture details.

Use sanitised workflow diagrams and illustrative data.

Do not invent:
- Business results.
- Performance metrics.
- User adoption figures.
- Governance approvals.
- Security controls.
- Technical integrations.
- Personal responsibilities.

Preserve the distinction between what I personally built, what I assessed, what the wider team implemented and what was recommended.

Where an impact figure is an estimate, label it appropriately.

Where a governance control was assessed but not implemented, do not imply it was implemented.

The finished website should feel like a credible portfolio from a hands-on AI implementation professional who understands both business problems and technical delivery.

A recruiter should be able to understand the portfolio in under two minutes.

- The business problem.
- What I personally contributed.

- How the systems were integrated.

Prioritise clarity, evidence and implementation detail over visual complexity.
